Call handling standards

Baseline expectations for how TSRs open, manage, and close customer calls.

Opening the call

  • Answer within the target hold time and greet with your name and department.
  • Confirm the account holder's identity before discussing account details.
  • Set expectations early: briefly restate the customer's issue back to them so they know you understood it correctly.

Example opening:

"Thanks for holding, this is [name] with CityWest technical support. Just to confirm, you're seeing buffering on your living room TV since last night — is that right?"

During the call

  • Own the call. Avoid unnecessary transfers; attempt standard troubleshooting before considering escalation.
  • Narrate what you're doing. Silence during diagnostics reads as inattention — tell the customer what you're checking and why.
  • Set time expectations. If a step will take a few minutes (e.g. a modem reboot), say so upfront.
  • Avoid jargon. Say "signal strength" instead of "SNR," describe steps in plain terms.
  • One issue at a time. If a customer raises a second, unrelated issue mid-call, acknowledge it and address it after resolving the first, rather than troubleshooting both simultaneously.

Hold procedure

  • Ask permission before placing a customer on hold, and give a reason ("I need about two minutes to check your line levels").
  • Check back in every 60–90 seconds if a hold runs longer than expected.
  • Thank the customer for their patience when returning.

Closing the call

  • Confirm the original issue is resolved from the customer's perspective, not just from the system's.
  • Summarize any follow-up actions (e.g. a technician visit, a callback, a firmware update rolling out).
  • Provide a reference/ticket number if one was created.
  • Ask if there's anything else before ending the call.

Example closing:

"So to recap, I've reset your set-top box and confirmed your signal levels are healthy — you should see clear picture going forward. If it happens again, reference ticket #12345. Anything else I can help with today?"

Documentation standards

  • Log every call, even if resolved quickly, with the specific symptom and the resolution steps taken.
  • Use consistent terminology so other TSRs and Tier 2 can follow the history without guessing.
  • Note signal levels, error counts, or other diagnostic readings at the time of the call — not just "checked signal, looked fine."

What to avoid

  • Guessing at a cause without checking it (e.g. blaming Wi-Fi without running a speed test).
  • Placing a customer on hold without explanation.
  • Ending a call before confirming the customer's issue is actually resolved.
  • Promising a fix timeline you can't confirm (e.g. "it'll be fixed by tomorrow" for a line issue you haven't escalated yet).
